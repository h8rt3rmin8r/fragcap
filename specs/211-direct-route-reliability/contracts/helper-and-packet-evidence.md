# Helper and Packet Evidence Contract

An admitted selected flow records `selected`, `proxy-attempt`, and a one-use receipt. Helper completion records its exact stage and elapsed time. Success records `proxy-connected`; failure records a typed reason and the actual I/O kind where applicable. A helper registered before receipt consumption reports `unconsumed` if admission fails; only actual ledger consumption reports `consumed`. A pending first packet lost after owner change or shutdown records both aggregate loss and `selected-packet-disposition: lost`. A selected first packet successfully returned to Windows after helper failure records `selected-packet-disposition: reinjected` and keeps the receipt unconsumed. The new reinjected disposition uses route-event evidence version 3; prior consumed and lost records remain version 2. If that return fails, it records `lost`. Neither state claims passive original-packet presence.

The selected flow carries the exact process stage role proved at admission. A direct executable can be `target`; a launcher chain's final client is `client`. Packet-owner equality uses that role. Helper-facing proxy facts normalize the final game as `client` for exact-game inspection. A matching tuple, digest, and PID with a different stage role cannot establish an observed-owned original.

A registered helper's live proxy-inspection authority ends at retirement. Its exact helper-to-proxy association remains bounded historical evidence for an already accepted proxy connection until artifact reconciliation. Historical lookup requires the exact transport, helper source, proxy destination, accept instant inside the registration window, and accept no later than retirement. A later connection cannot borrow it; overlapping active and retired matches are explicitly ambiguous. The historical association does not establish client transfer or passive original-packet presence.

The managed packet gate normally requires the packet instant inside the ETW causal interval. A native direct-route attestation proves one exact selected first packet and its process generation independently. That first packet may pass before ETW publication if its role is retained and the capture window, direction, and volume bounds allow it. This exception never promotes an ordinary socket-table packet or a same-tuple later packet.

The worker does not wait for SOCKS CONNECT. Its pending state is bounded by exact tuple and endpoint generation. A duplicate selected packet is either retained under the same pending owner or explicitly accounted; it cannot begin a second helper. Session stop closes pending helper work within its finite deadline and retires receipts.

Route reachability requires actual client transfer. Passive original-packet presence is an independent field. Original-absent and incomplete-history states never become original-present because a helper packet or later same-tuple packet was observed.

The capture pipeline may prefer packet-specific proof over a missing or stale socket-table owner only if a complete datagram's full network-datagram digest or a digest omitting only the IPv4 header and TCP/UDP checksum fields, tuple, and capture instant match exactly one route-selected first packet. Checksum normalization accounts for the different capture planes under Windows checksum offload; IP identity, transport header, options, and payload remain in the accepted digest. The route-selected process generation and terminal role must still agree, and the managed gate enforces the retained role, capture window, direction, and volume bounds. The output marks this evidence `attr=direct-route`, never `live`. Missing, truncated, time-mismatched, digest-mismatched, or competing evidence remains unresolved. Capture-side aggregate counts distinguish tuple observations, time mismatches, incomplete packets, digest mismatches, ambiguous matches, exact matches, and checksum-normalized matches without storing raw bytes or digests in diagnostic text. Separate IP-ID-normalized and transport-only comparisons count diagnostic near matches but never grant attribution.

When process or socket evidence arrives after Npcap, a flow-bearing managed direct-route packet can wait in a separate finite output decision lane for at most 250 ms. Due retries recheck socket ownership and the exact packet attestor without weakening digest uniqueness. Expiry or lane-capacity displacement records a gate drop and flow-local omission, not a positive original. The lane may write a deferred packet after a later captured packet within that decision window. Acquisition-stage attributed and unattributed counters describe the initial lookup; the final retained owner belongs to the packet record and flow observation. The selected flow retains at most 32 uniquely attested capture digests; overflow records incomplete history, and same-tuple overlapping selections remain ambiguous.

Candidate resolution indexes socket events by tuple so traffic for other sockets does not multiply each held packet's scan cost. A new packet checks only its own proof; held candidates are reexamined when socket or process evidence changes and otherwise expire by deadline in enqueue order. Cleanup records the largest candidate queue delay, the largest held duration, and the count held over 50 ms. These are diagnostic facts; they do not waive the physical unrelated-traffic limit.

The receive channel reserves 16 of its 128 candidate slots against unknown UDP traffic. The worker's 256-packet candidate buffer reserves one eighth of packet and byte capacity for TCP SYNs and holds at most eight unproven UDP packets per tuple. A rejected candidate returns its original bytes to the existing counted pass-through path. These finite limits address a reproduced target SYN capacity bypass under an unknown-owner UDP burst; they do not guarantee selection under arbitrary TCP SYN floods or preserve all UDP datagrams before socket ownership is published.

While a selected TCP helper connects, only the exact proxy listener or a packet with positively known Fragcap helper PID bypasses the candidate path as infrastructure. An unknown owner for another destination remains a bounded candidate; later socket and managed-process proof may bind it to a separate game endpoint generation. Unknown ownership never admits routing by itself. The earlier portable physical matrix used one selected flow per attempt, so concurrent admission currently has controlled source evidence only.

A selected route binds its tuple, endpoint identifier, PID, and socket OPEN QPC as one observed socket epoch. Its packet timestamp must follow that OPEN. The socket observer retains bounded exact `[OPEN, CLOSE)` intervals for recently closed epochs; a packet received or dequeued after a later socket event may still enter its old selected route only when its QPC falls in one unique observed interval. The receive thread registers selected work under the route lock before queueing it. A matching CLOSE or competing OPEN stops new live selection, retires the old receipt, and briefly retains the old TCP bridge or UDP association for already admitted pre-retirement packets. Neither old bridge becomes the new owner's route. Synthetic replies require a still-live exact old owner; a reply that cannot safely target it is counted and recorded as withheld. A bounded session-long tuple quarantine counts late selected TCP packets lost if finite owner history or bridge state expires, rather than passing them raw to a reused connection. An owner-unproven outbound UDP datagram returns its unchanged original bytes and WinDivert address once as explicit uninspected pass-through, without positive old-owner, proxy-transfer, or client-delivery evidence. A failed return remains Lost. The remote may see a different source endpoint from the earlier proxy relay, so peer acceptance is not claimed. A returned-data ACK witness remains bound to the original epoch and observed close time.

An exact retained old CLOSE can identify that an uninspected UDP datagram followed the selected tuple's prior route, even though it cannot establish who owned the packet. That case records a conservative per-flow omission as **tuple lineage only** and marks the route report limited. If the old route is not unique or another owner is proved, only aggregate uncertainty is recorded; no packet is attributed to the prior process. Distinct aggregate labels separate exact post-CLOSE timing from missing or ambiguous owner proof. The external peer accepts a UDP finish for qualification only from the same source endpoint as its routed request.

A header-only TCP FIN/RST after the exact old socket CLOSE remains counted Lost with a distinct post-close control-tail reason only when the observer retains that exact CLOSE and neither historical nor live lookup names another owner. A separate ACK-only diagnostic requires exact TCP flags `0x10`, a complete header with zero payload, packet QPC strictly after the exact retained CLOSE, absent historical and live owner results, and unique prior selected tuple lineage. It remains Lost and unattributed; the observed absence does not prove that the packet belonged to the old client. A missing close, equal or earlier packet QPC, SYN, payload, or ambiguous owner keeps the generic loss reason. The controlled fixture may accept at most one exactly classified ACK-only loss per case alongside the FIN/RST class, while failing on any generic selected loss. Neither class establishes loss-free delivery.

WinDivert's network and socket handles do not supply an atomic packet-and-owner snapshot. A competing socket event still in flight on the socket handle may become visible only after a network packet was delivered. A unique *observed* epoch therefore cannot by itself prove actual owner identity at interception across that interval. The historical interval repair handles observed event reordering, while unresolved event loss or an in-flight event remains a limited outcome. #594's exact-candidate physical packet and artifact matrix remains open; the earlier portable hashes establish only their recorded one-selected-flow cases.

The portable package matrix checks original-present and original-absent orderings, incomplete packet history, TCP and UDP transfer and refusal, and a separate connected TCP zero-byte case against the full route journal, compatibility, terminal result, and manifest. The zero-byte case uses a finite local peer and is distinct from the eight-case external-peer packet matrix. Controlled source tests exercise tuple reuse, ambiguous or lost socket evidence, and bounded history without claiming those cases ran in the packaged matrix. Each outcome must remain distinct and reconciled at its tested evidence boundary. A controlled result does not assert an installed ESO result.
