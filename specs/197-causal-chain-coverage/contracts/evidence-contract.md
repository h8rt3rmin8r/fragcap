# Contract: Causal Launch-Chain Evidence

## Operator input and plan

`--inspect-scope auto|game|launch-chain` remains the public vocabulary. New managed `auto` resolves to `launch-chain` application inspection for every topology. The pre-effect plan names requested scope, effective application scope, full causal-chain packet scope, route, inherited-route release window, exact descendant process-control class, and independently authorized retry or fallback. Explicit `game` changes application handling only.

## Exact compatibility and resume

Positive facts require the existing exact target, launch case, route, address family, backend, version, protocol, effective scope, middleware handling, and current policy identity. Historical rows remain displayable. A resumed workflow with stale effective scope, middleware handling, or policy identity refuses before effects with fresh-workflow guidance.

## Packet artifact and correlation

The packet file retains original order and timestamps for admitted packets. Coverage metadata states launch, client observation, route-owner release, and final packet collection instants separately. A withheld packet and correlated application connection report actual waiting, out-of-window, out-of-scope, or unresolved reason. Capacity and source failures have separate named loss. Proxy-only evidence after an early packet stop remains explicitly uncorrelated.

## Managed shutdown and recovery

The plan authorizes process control only for an exact session-created root or causally proven descendant with complete generation, path, account, session, and receipt proof. At observation end or another terminal condition, the finite graceful/force schedule applies to every proven live instance and verifies exit. Incomplete proof or survival leaves release incomplete; journal and Doctor preserve historical failure and current recoverability.

## Compatibility guarantees

Ordinary Capture and unmodified packet analyzers retain their contracts. Direct raw sockets remain packet-observable without becoming routed or inspected. A client-facing TLS refusal remains a failure absent separate exact success evidence. An established connection never switches from inspection to relay after failure.
